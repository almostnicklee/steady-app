## 1. Comparable products and features (what they measure, real-time vs after, evidence, status in 2026)

### Takeaway
The market has repeatedly tried "live private nudges" on observable speech behavior (pace, filler words, volume, interruptions, talk time) and, separately, emotion or sentiment inference. The emotion-inference products have mostly retreated, been absorbed, or shut down: Microsoft retired Azure emotion recognition (2022), Amazon Halo with its voice "Tone" feature was discontinued (2023), Teams Speaker Coach was retired without reaching general availability (Aug 2025), Woebot's consumer app closed (June 2025), Poised shut down (Oct 2026), and Cogito was folded into Verint (2024). Effectiveness evidence for nearly all commercial tools is vendor-reported, not independent or peer-reviewed.

### Inferences
- The products that survived or grew (Yoodli, PowerPoint rehearsal coach, sales conversation intelligence) measure observable delivery behaviors (pace, fillers, talk ratio) rather than labeled emotions; the ones built around inferred emotion (Halo Tone, Azure emotion, Zoom's research) were retired or drew the strongest backlash. Steady's safest product shape is behavior-level cues, not emotion verdicts.
- Teams Speaker Coach's retirement "after evaluating feedback and usage" suggests that even a free, private, first-party live coach struggled with sustained adoption; Steady should plan for engagement drop-off and measure it.
- Halo Tone is the closest precedent to Steady (on-device, self-directed, about how you come across). It failed on perceived usefulness and accuracy, not only privacy, so local processing alone does not make the product acceptable.
- Cogito shows how a "self-improvement" coach turns into a monitoring tool once dashboards reach supervisors. Steady's no-sharing architecture is a key differentiator and should be kept non-negotiable.
- Shutdowns (Poised, Woebot) deleted user histories; a local-only app avoids that, but users may still want export.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed evaluation was found for Cogito, Poised, Yoodli, Orai, Speaker Coach, Zoom Revenue Accelerator, Gong or Chorus. All effectiveness numbers found are vendor- or customer-reported.
- Gong/Chorus sentiment features: no source found describing how sentiment is computed or validated.
- Orai's current operating status could not be confirmed.
- Whether Hume's Expression Measurement API is still offered unchanged in late 2026 was not confirmed.
- No meta-analysis specifically on anger-management apps was found; pooled effect sizes for the JITAI and reappraisal meta-analyses were not visible in snippets.
- Smart Eye/Affectiva product changes after 2021 were not found.

## 2. Research on real-time feedback during conversations: benefits, distraction costs, user acceptance

### Takeaway
Small controlled studies show that sparse, private, behavior-level cues can change speaking behavior without derailing the speaker (Rhema, Meeting Mediator), and that real-time AI assistance can improve outcomes, especially for less experienced people, and may produce learning (Brynjolfsson et al.). The Superpower Glass RCT showed modest gains for autistic children using emotion cues. The main documented failure modes are over-frequent alerts, misreads of context (a shared laugh flagged as talking over), and the cue becoming a supervisor's metric.

### Cited Findings
- **Rhema (Univ. of Rochester, IUI 2015):** Google Glass gave live feedback on volume and speaking rate; within-subject study with 30 native English speakers plus Mechanical Turk audience raters. A sparse scheme (a word like "louder"/"slower" shown for ~3 seconds, then blank for ~20 seconds; nothing shown if the speaker is doing well) beat continuous feedback: speakers were significantly more satisfied (p<0.05). Researchers warned that enough distraction can cause stuttering or awkward pauses; speakers maintained good eye contact in all conditions per the press release — [Rochester news release](https://www.rochester.edu/newscenter/wearable-technology-can-help-with-public-speaking-95552); [Tanveer, Lin, Hoque 2015 paper](https://www.hoques.com/Publications/2015/2015-Rhema-IUI-Tanveer-Lin-Hoque.pdf); [ROC Speak Glass PDF](https://cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/rocspeakglass.pdf)
- **Meeting Mediator (MIT Media Lab, ~2008):** Sociometric badges sensed speech, movement and proximity, with feedback shown privately on each participant's phone rather than a public screen. In a brainstorming/problem-solving study it significantly reduced overlapping speech and changed interactivity "without distracting the subjects"; a later study found it increased speaking time and turn transitions in distributed groups and raised cooperation. Earlier work (DiMicco et al.) with public speaking-time displays produced more equal participation — [MIT Media Lab publication](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/meeting-mediator-enhancing-group-collaboration-with-sociometric-feedback-2); [Personal and Ubiquitous Computing article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00779-010-0284-x)
- **Generative AI at Work (Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond; QJE 2025, 140(2):889–942):** 5,179 customer-support agents received real-time suggested responses they could ignore or edit. Productivity (issues resolved/hour) rose 14% on average in the NBER version (15% in the later version), with a 34% gain for novice/low-skilled workers and minimal effect for experienced workers. AI assistance improved customer sentiment, increased employee retention and "may lead to worker learning"; customers were more polite and less likely to ask for a manager — [NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161); [Stanford Digital Economy Lab](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/generative-ai-at-work); [arXiv 2304.11771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771)
- **Cogito in practice (NYT reporting, 2019, via secondary sources):** Several MetLife employees said they liked the pop-ups, but some "struggled to figure out how to get the 'empathy' notification to stop appearing"; one said the empathy cue pinged too often, and the tool once flagged a rep and customer as talking over each other when they were sharing a laugh. Kevin Roose described it as "a kind of adjunct manager, always watching." At Humana use was optional, but the company was considering tying it to bonuses — [Santa Clara Univ. case](https://scu.edu/ethics/focus-areas/internet-ethics/resources/sentio-ergo-sum); [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2019-08-ai-machines-human-workers-compassionate.amp)
- **Superpower Glass RCT (Voss et al., JAMA Pediatrics, March 2019):** 71 children with autism (mean age 8.4; 89% boys), 40 treatment vs 31 control (one summary says 41/30). Glass + phone app detected faces' expressions and gave social cues; 20-minute home sessions 4x/week for 6 weeks plus standard ABA therapy. The treatment group improved more on Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales socialization (about 4.5 points). Authors could not rule out practice effects for some gains; several authors had financial ties/patents licensed to Cognoa. Trial NCT03569176 — [JAMA Pediatrics abstract](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/article-abstract/2728462); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6503634); [NIBIB](https://www.nibib.nih.gov/news-events/newsroom/super-tool-helps-kids-autism-improve-socialization-skills); [Psychiatric News](https://psychnews.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.pn.2019.5a14)
- **Emotion regulation science relevant to what to coach:** Cognitive reappraisal has a healthier profile of short-term affective, cognitive and social consequences than expressive suppression. Suppression intervenes late, inhibiting display of an emotion already generated; in Goldin & Gross's imaging work suppression increased amygdala activity while reappraisal reduced negative emotion across self-report, facial and neural measures. Findings are not uniform, and a 2024 paper calls suppression's experiential effects unclear — [Cutuli 2014 review (PMC4168764)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4168764/); [PSU Psych 425 chapter](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/response-modulation-expressive-suppression/); [PubMed 39503344](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39503344/)
- **Affect labeling:** Lieberman et al. (2007) found that labeling one's own affect diminished amygdala response to negative images, via a right ventrolateral PFC to medial PFC pathway; later work found affect labeling also dampens pleasure from positive images, and a 2021 study found emotion naming impeded reappraisal — [PubMed 17576282](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576282/); [Lieberman 2007 PDF](https://sites.lifesci.ucla.edu/psych-sanlab/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2016/03/Lieberman_2007_PsychScience.pdf)

### Inferences
- Design evidence points to sparse cues (Rhema's ~3s on / ~20s off), silence when things are fine, single-word or icon cues, and a display only the user sees (Meeting Mediator, Yoodli). Continuous meters are worse.
- Real-time help seems to benefit novices most (Brynjolfsson). For Steady, that supports a "scaffold that fades" design: frequent cues early, tapering as the user internalizes the skill, which matches the stated goal of eventually not needing the app. This is an inference; no study tested fading for emotion regulation at work.
- Cue content matters: a cue that pushes the user to hide emotion (suppression, "stop frowning") may raise physiological arousal and hurt memory during the meeting, while cues that prompt a pause, breath, or reframing (reappraisal) have better evidence. Prompting users to name their own state (affect labeling) is evidence-backed and keeps the user, not the model, as the authority on their feelings.
- Cogito's experience shows that false positives (a laugh read as conflict) and nagging alerts erode trust; Steady needs easy snooze/dismiss, adjustable sensitivity, and a "that was wrong" button.

### Gaps
- No study was found that tests real-time emotion-regulation cues for adults during real workplace meetings; evidence is from public speaking (Rhema), lab groups (Meeting Mediator), call centers (Cogito, Brynjolfsson) and autistic children (Superpower Glass).
- Bailenson's 2021 "nonverbal overload" / mirror-anxiety work on self-view in video calls (relevant to a camera-based coach increasing self-consciousness) did not appear in search results; not verified.
- Long-term retention or transfer (does behavior persist without the cue?) is largely untested, except the Brynjolfsson "may lead to worker learning" finding.
- Superpower Glass user acceptance or dropout details were not visible in snippets.

## 3. Criticisms and failure modes of emotion AI

### Takeaway
The scientific basis for reading emotion from faces and voices is contested (Barrett et al. 2019), regulators and major vendors have called the field immature (UK ICO 2022, Microsoft 2022), and documented biases affect race, gender and neurodivergent users. Critics also warn about normalizing surveillance and pushing people to perform "correct" affect. These concerns apply even to self-directed tools because the model's labels can still be wrong in systematically biased ways.

### Cited Findings
- **Science:** Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez and Pollak (Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 2019) reviewed more than 1,000 studies and challenged the assumption of a reliable mapping between facial configurations and specific emotions (e.g., a smile can signal submission rather than happiness); APS noted such software was being deployed for surveillance, hiring, clinical diagnosis and market research — [PMC6640856](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6640856); [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/emotion-expressions-pspi.html)
- **AI Now 2019 report:** First recommendation: regulators should ban affect recognition in important decisions that affect people's lives and access to opportunities, and until then companies should stop deploying it, because it lacks "any solid scientific foundation" — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2019/12/13/131585/emotion-recognition-technology-should-be-banned-says-ai-research-institute/); [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/201912/ai-now-calls-for-ban-on-affect-recognition-as-market-expected-to-surge-to-90b-by-2024)
- **Regulator warning:** In October 2022 the UK ICO issued its first general warning about a whole technology, saying emotion analysis is "immature… may not work yet, or indeed ever" (Deputy Commissioner Stephen Bonner), that risks outweigh opportunities, and that it would investigate irresponsible use; it cited systemic bias, inaccuracy and discrimination — [ICO news release](https://cy.ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2022/10/immature-biometric-technologies-could-be-discriminating-against-people-says-ico-in-warning-to-organisations); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/b/ico-warns-organizations-about-use-of-emotion-analysis-tech); [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/252526548/ICO-warns-against-using-biometrics-for-emotional-analysis)
- **Racial bias:** Lauren Rhue ran ~400 NBA player photos through Face++ and Microsoft Face API: Face++ scored Black players as angrier than white players even controlling for smiling; Microsoft scored Black faces as angrier/more contemptuous when expressions were ambiguous, with the gap disappearing as smiles widened. Coverage reported Face++ rated Black faces about twice as angry on average and Face API three times more contemptuous — [SSRN working paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3281765); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/emotion-reading-tech-fails-the-racial-bias-test-108404); [Singularity Hub](https://singularityhub.com/2019/01/11/understanding-the-hidden-bias-in-emotion-reading-ais/); [UMD Smith School](https://www.rhsmith.umd.edu/research/race-factor-facial-recognition-tech)
- **Disability, culture, language:** The Fight for the Future coalition said emotion tools may misread people with disabilities, other cultures and non-native English speakers, and argued that emotion AI is pseudoscientific, discriminatory, and can be used punitively — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/enterprise-software/news/252518128/Privacy-groups-urge-Zoom-to-abandon-emotion-AI-research); [Fight for the Future](https://www.fightforthefuture.org/news/2022-05-10-letter-to-zoom/)
- **Neurodivergent users:** An audit project ("Smile ≠ Happy") argues emotion recognition models rely on neurotypical expression standards, so autistic people with atypical facial affect, reduced eye contact or lower expressivity may be misclassified — [Univ. of Bologna course project](https://apice.unibo.it/xwiki/bin/view/Courseproject/KefaliAliWoreta2425)
- A 2025 paper on neuro-inclusive AI reports speech emotion recognition can pathologize autistic communication, and hiring tools have read lack of eye contact as lack of confidence — [arXiv 2507.21077](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21077)
- Emotion-model training labels typically reflect annotators' perceptions, not what the person felt — [arXiv 2507.21077](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21077)
- **Gender bias:** A University of Washington study examined gender bias in the tones Amazon Halo ascribed to voices — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10517278)
- **Surveillance creep:** Cogito's "empathy" alerts fed a supervisor dashboard; minimizing the window alerted a supervisor; Humana considered linking it to bonuses — [Santa Clara Univ. case](https://scu.edu/ethics/focus-areas/internet-ethics/resources/sentio-ergo-sum)
- **Vendor self-regulation:** The Hume Initiative guidelines explicitly require "Scientific Legitimacy," "Inclusivity," testing in each deployed population, transparency and consent — [Hume Initiative](https://hume-initiative-website.pages.dev/guidelines); [Hume docs](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/resources/use-case-guidelines)

### Inferences
- If Steady flags "anger" from a face or voice, it will likely flag Black users, users with atypical prosody, autistic users, and non-native speakers more often for the same behavior. That both harms users and creates discrimination risk. Measuring only user-relative change from the user's own baseline, and avoiding categorical emotion labels, reduces (but does not eliminate) this.
- A tool aimed at people with regulation difficulties overlaps heavily with neurodivergent populations (ADHD, autism). A coach that pushes neurotypical expression norms could amount to automated masking pressure. The framing should be "notice your own early warning signs" rather than "look calmer."
- Self-consciousness risk: constant face/voice self-monitoring during meetings may increase anxiety for the very users it targets; this needs user testing and an easy "audio-only" or "words-only" mode.
- Even a private tool can normalize workplace emotion monitoring if employers later suggest or mandate it; Steady's terms and design should resist employer deployment (e.g., no admin console, no team plans).

### Gaps
- No source quantified racial or cultural bias in voice-based (prosody) emotion models specifically; Rhue's work is face-only and from 2018 models.
- No direct source on masking/camouflaging as a confound for emotion AI was found.
- No empirical study on whether self-monitoring coaches increase self-consciousness or anxiety was found (Bailenson self-view work not verified).
- EFF and Ada Lovelace Institute reports on emotion AI did not surface in searches.

## 4. Legal and regulatory landscape (as of November 2026): settled vs uncertain

### Takeaway
The biggest legal risk is the EU: Article 5(1)(f) of the AI Act bans emotion-inference AI "in the areas of workplace and education" (in force since 2 Feb 2025, unchanged by the 2026 Digital Omnibus). The only route for Steady is a non-binding Commission-guidelines carve-out for personal training with no HR sharing, which commentators say has no basis in the Act's text. In the US, on-device processing with no vendor access is a strong but not conclusive defense under BIPA (Seventh Circuit, Aug 2026); all-party-consent wiretap laws, Washington's MHMDA, FTC substantiation rules and FDA claim boundaries are the other live issues.

### Cited Findings

**EU AI Act**
- Article 5(1)(f) prohibits AI systems that infer emotions in the workplace or educational institutions, except for medical or safety reasons. The Commission's guidelines list examples such as call centres using webcams and voice recognition to track employees' emotions (e.g., anger), emotion recognition during recruitment and probation, and monitoring emotional tone in hybrid team video calls. Tracking customers' emotions falls outside the ban. The guidelines are non-binding; only the CJEU gives authoritative interpretations — [CMS: Commission guidelines Part II](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-commission-issues-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-part-ii); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/04/EU-Commission-Publishes-Guidelines-on-the-Prohibited-AI-Practices-under-the-AI-Act); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/european-commission-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-under-the-eu-artificial-intelligence-act/)
- **Personal-training carve-out (key for Steady):** Commentary on the guidelines says emotion recognition used only for an employee's personal training may be allowed if results are not shared with HR, cannot affect assessment or promotion, and the ban is not circumvented. Commentators note this exemption "is nowhere to be found in the provisions or the recitals of the Act itself" and was suggested rather than formally codified. Recruitment and probation remain prohibited, and general monitoring of wellbeing, stress or burnout does not qualify as a "medical" use. Sentiment analysis of written text is outside the ban — [Technology's Legal Edge](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/2025/04/eu-ai-act-spotlight-on-emotional-recognition-systems-in-the-workplace/); [Oliver Patel](https://oliverpatel.substack.com/p/emotion-recognition-and-the-eu-ai); [Reglog](https://reglog.io/blog/eu-ai-act-emotion-recognition-workplace); [Praxikon](https://www.praxikon.com/en/posts/emotion-recognition-workplace-ban-ai-act) (snippet-level; which commentator said what could not be attributed per source)
- **Definition:** Article 3(39) defines an "emotion recognition system" as AI identifying or inferring emotions or intentions "on the basis of their biometric data." Recital 18 gives examples (frown, smile, hand/head gestures, raised voice, whispering), and excludes physical states such as pain or fatigue and the mere detection of readily apparent expressions, gestures or movements, provided the system does not infer emotional states. The AI Act's "biometric data" is broader than the GDPR's because it need not allow unique identification. Inference of "intentions" appears to be left out of the 5(1)(f) prohibition — [AI Act Service Desk: Recital 18](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/recital-18); [artificialintelligenceact.eu Recital 18](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/18/); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/global/what-is-an-emotion-recognition-system-under-the-eus-artificial-intelligence-act-part-1); [Wolters Kluwer blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/global-workplace-law-and-policy/the-prohibition-of-ai-emotion-recognition-technologies-in-the-workplace-under-the-ai-act/); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/)
- Emotion recognition not prohibited by Article 5 is high-risk under Annex III point 1(c) (subject to the Article 6(3) "no significant risk" carve-out), and Article 50(3) requires deployers to inform people "exposed to" emotion recognition; compliance with 50(3) does not cure a prohibited deployment — [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/feeling-watched-transparency-obligations-for-emotion-recognition-and); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/)
- A Commission draft guideline on Annex III high-risk classification (with a section on point 1(c)) circulated in May 2026; only its table of contents surfaced — [Draft Annex III guidelines PDF](https://www.dirittobancario.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Draft-Guidelines-on-the-classification-of-high-risk-AI-Annex-III.pdf)
- **Digital Omnibus on AI:** Provisional agreement May 2026; published as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026, in force 27 July. It postpones high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I) and adds new Article 5 bans on non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM from 2 Dec 2026. The original Article 5 practices, including 5(1)(f), are unchanged and have applied since 2 Feb 2025; top fines are up to EUR 35M or 7% of worldwide turnover — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/digital-omnibus-on-ai-provisional-agreement-reached-at-the-may-trilogue); [NicFab](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/digital-omnibus-ai-official-journal/); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)

**US biometric laws**
- **Illinois BIPA, on-device:** In G.T. v. Samsung (7th Cir., August 2026) the court read BIPA's "possess," "collect," "capture" and "obtain" to require some control over the biometric data and affirmed dismissal where face templates stayed on users' phones and Samsung could not access, modify or use them; commentators call it binding precedent in the circuit for on-device processing. An earlier Windows face-scanning case was dismissed by distinguishing control of software from control of scan data — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/seventh-circuit-holds-that-bipa-does-not-reach-biometric-data-that-remains-on-a-users-device); [Duane Morris](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/08/21/the-class-action-weekly-wire-episode-161-seventh-circuit-upholds-dismissal-of-biometric-privacy-class-action/); [Washington Legal Foundation](https://www.wlf.org/2026/09/01/wlf-legal-pulse/possess-or-collect-under-illinois-bipa-seventh-circuit-resolves-trial-court-split/)
- Counterpoint: in the Apple Photos case possession claims survived the pleading stage on Apple's processing; a later report says faceprint claims went to a jury while "face crops" were ruled not biometric — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/bipa-lawsuit-proceeds-against-apple-in-federal-court); [Recording Law](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/apple-photos-bipa-face-crops-ruling/)

- AI product features are a growing target in BIPA litigation — [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://classaction.fkks.com/post/102o43p/ai-product-features-are-a-growing-target-in-bipa-litigation)
- **Texas CUBI:** AG-only enforcement, up to $25,000 per violation; Meta settled for $1.4B (July 30, 2024) over facial recognition in tagging, and Google for $1.375B over biometric and geolocation claims — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/meta-settles-texas-biometric-data-lawsuit-for-record-1-4-billion); [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/ftc-state-ag/dont-mess-with-texas-cubi-settlement-marks-state-tough-biometric-privacy-enforcer/); [CSLR on Google](https://www.cslawreport.com/21169961/texas-ags-billiondollar-settlement-with-google-highlights-biometric-data-use-compliance-considerations.thtml); [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/07/texas-wins-14-billion-biometric-settlement-against-meta-it-would-have-happened)
- **Washington RCW 19.375:** prohibits enrolling a biometric identifier "in a database for a commercial purpose" without notice, consent or an opt-out mechanism; "enroll" means capturing, converting to a template, and storing in a database linked to an individual; "commercial purpose" means furthering sale or disclosure to a third party — [RCW 19.375.010](https://apps.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.375.010); [RCW 19.375.020](https://apps.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.375.020)
- **Colorado HB24-1130** (C.R.S. 6-1-1314) added biometric rules to the Colorado Privacy Act, effective July 1, 2025, for controllers processing any amount of biometric identifiers, with annual retention review — [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202506/texas-and-colorado-take-diverging-paths-on-biometric-privacy); [Baker Donelson chart](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/webfiles/Publications/Global-Biometrics-Laws-Chart.pdf)

**Wiretap / recording consent and transcription**
- California Penal Code 632 requires all parties' consent to record a confidential communication; Kearney v. Salomon Smith Barney (2006) applies it to interstate calls with a California party. ### Inferences
- **EU (highest risk, unsettled):** Steady infers emotional states from face and voice (biometric data) and is used in work meetings, which fits 5(1)(f) on its face. The personal-training carve-out matches Steady's architecture (no HR sharing, no assessment), but it sits only in non-binding guidance. A regulator or the CJEU could disagree, and the Act also bans "placing on the market" for that purpose, which reaches Steady as provider. Options: (a) geofence face/voice emotion inference out of the EU; (b) in the EU, offer only words-only or observable-behavior features (pace, volume, interruptions) that detect "readily apparent" signals without inferring emotion (Recital 18), though if the product's purpose is emotion regulation a regulator may treat it as inference anyway; (c) take EU legal advice. The medical exception would require a genuine medical purpose and conflicts with a wellness positioning.
- **Article 2(10)**'s exemption for purely personal, non-professional use by individuals likely does not help, because use in work meetings is professional activity; and it addresses deployers' obligations, not providers' (unverified, see Gaps).
- **BIPA:** G.T. v. Samsung strongly supports an architecture where face processing happens in the browser and Steady receives no templates, landmarks, derived scores or telemetry. It binds federal courts in the Seventh Circuit (which covers Illinois), but Illinois state courts are not bound on state-law interpretation. Any analytics that send derived face data to Steady's servers would weaken the defense.
- **Wiretap:** If Steady transcribes other participants' speech, that may count as interception or recording in all-party states even on-device. Otter's loss turned on vendor commercial use (training). A tool that keeps transcripts ephemeral, never transmits them, and never trains on them is closer to the Graham v. Noom "extension of the user" theory, but no case has resolved real-time, non-stored, on-device transcription. Safer defaults: analyze only the user's own mic channel and own words; if others' audio is processed, prompt the user to disclose and obtain consent.
- **MHMDA:** Emotion-regulation inferences likely count as "consumer health data" (mental health status; behavioral interventions; derived inferences). Whether Steady "collects" data that never leaves the device is untested. A compliant consumer health data privacy policy and affirmative consent flow are cheap insurance given the private right of action.
- **FTC:** Being free does not exempt Steady from Section 5. Claims like "reduces outbursts," "clinically proven," or accuracy percentages need competent and reliable evidence, and health-adjacent claims face a higher bar (likely RCT-level). If Steady stores any health-related data and shares it (even via analytics SDKs), the HBNR's "unauthorized disclosure" concept could apply.
- **FDA:** Keep claims within general wellness (communication habits, stress awareness, workplace composure). Avoid claims to diagnose, treat or mitigate anger disorders, intermittent explosive disorder, ADHD, BPD, anxiety or depression. Woebot shows the FDA pathway is costly for a small team.

### Gaps
- Exact text and paragraph number of the Commission guidelines' personal-training language could not be read (fetch blocked); only commentary was seen.
- Whether a self-directed tool used by a worker on their own initiative falls within the "workplace" concept of 5(1)(f), and how self-employed users are treated, was not found.
- How Article 2(10) applies to individuals using Steady at work was not found in any source.
- No case law on whether ephemeral, on-device, real-time transcription without storage is a "recording" under CIPA 632 or other all-party statutes.
- No MHMDA ruling on whether purely on-device processing is "collection" by the app provider; Maxwell v. Amazon status unknown.
- Whether RCW 19.375 excludes photographs/video, and whether emotion inference from facial landmarks (without identification) is a "scan of face geometry" under BIPA, were not resolved in sources found.
- Whether the 2024 HBNR has been challenged in court was not found.
- The specific January 2026 FDA guidance wording on mental or behavioral wellness claims (versus stress) was not visible; the FDA document itself (fda.gov/media/90652/download) could not be fetched.
- FTC guidance specific to emotion AI claims was not found.

## 5. Design principles from responsible-AI and digital mental health guidance

### Takeaway
Across vendor ethics codes, regulators and digital mental health frameworks, the recurring principles are: user consent and control, transparency about what is measured, scientific humility (don't claim to read emotions), no sharing with employers, data minimization and local processing, validation across populations, clear crisis pathways, and positioning as a support, not therapy. Evidence on effective feedback adds: sparse, private, dismissible, behavior-level cues that favor reappraisal over suppression.

### Cited Findings
- The Hume Initiative's six principles (Beneficence, Emotional Primacy, Scientific Legitimacy, Inclusivity, Transparency, Consent) and its call to test empathic AI in each population where it is deployed — [Hume Initiative guidelines](https://hume-initiative-website.pages.dev/guidelines); [Hume developer docs](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/resources/use-case-guidelines)
- Microsoft retired emotion inference because of lack of scientific consensus on defining "emotions" and overgeneralization risks, keeping it only in controlled accessibility uses — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/microsoft-shutting-down-azure-facial-emotion-recognitio-1849090511); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/microsoft-removing-emotion-recognition-features-facial-recognition-tec-rcna35087)
- The ICO urged risk assessments before deploying emotion analysis and warned of bias, inaccuracy and discrimination — [ICO](https://cy.ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2022/10/immature-biometric-technologies-could-be-discriminating-against-people-says-ico-in-warning-to-organisations)

- APA's Woebot evaluation flagged that, despite a disclaimer that the app was not for crises, "there appear to be some functional questions around crisis features within the app" — [APA Woebot evaluation](https://psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/mental-health-apps/evaluations/woebot-evaluation)
- Evidence that in-app safety plans match clinic-derived plans and improve outcomes is lacking; clinicians are advised to test an app's emergency resources for accuracy before an emergency — [Focus (APA)](https://focus.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.focus.20220073)
- Self-guided apps still need professional monitoring for severe or complex presentations or safety concerns — [eMHPrac practitioner guide](https://emhprac.org.au/?p=6252)
- Feedback design evidence: sparse word cues preferred over continuous (Rhema); private phone displays reduced overlap without distraction (Meeting Mediator); live coaches that survive in market use private nudges only the user sees (Yoodli) — [Rochester](https://www.rochester.edu/newscenter/wearable-technology-can-help-with-public-speaking-95552); [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/meeting-mediator-enhancing-group-collaboration-with-sociometric-feedback-2); [Yoodli](https://yoodli.ai/use-cases/online-meetings)
- Reappraisal outperforms suppression on affective, cognitive and social outcomes; affect labeling by the person reduces amygdala reactivity — [PMC4168764](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4168764/); [PubMed 17576282](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576282/)
- Local processing alone did not save Amazon Halo Tone, which was criticized as inaccurate and not useful — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/lifestyle/gadgets/article/3099589/can-amazon-halo-can-tell-your-emotions-your-tone-voice-experts); [Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/culture/technology/2020/12/10/review-amazons-new-health-band-may-be-the-most-invasive-tech-ever)

### Inferences
Concrete design implications for Steady, synthesized from the above (inferences, not sourced rules):
- **Describe signals, not feelings.** Show "your voice got louder and faster" or "you've interrupted 3 times," not "you're angry." Let the user label their own state (affect labeling), which is evidence-backed and keeps the model from asserting emotions.
- **Personal baseline, not population norms.** Compare users to their own calm baseline to reduce cultural, racial and neurodivergent bias; let users mark false positives and adjust thresholds.
- **Sparse, private, dismissible cues** that go silent when things are fine; avoid continuous meters; offer a fade-out schedule so users practice self-detection without the app.
- **Coach regulation strategies (pause, breathe, reframe, ask a question), not suppression** ("relax your face").
- **Modality control:** let users turn face, voice and words analysis on or off separately; offer a no-camera mode for users who find self-monitoring stressful.
- **Data minimization by architecture:** in-browser inference, no server transmission of raw or derived face/voice/transcript data, no training on user data, ephemeral transcripts by default, optional local export/delete. This supports the BIPA (G.T. v. Samsung), wiretap (Otter distinction), MHMDA and EU personal-training positions.
- **No employer channel:** no admin dashboards, team plans or shareable scores, to stay within the EU carve-out conditions and avoid Cogito-style surveillance creep. Terms should prohibit employer-mandated use.
- **Consent for others:** if other participants' audio is processed, prompt disclosure; default to analyzing only the user's own mic.
- **Crisis and scope:** test crisis resources (by region), surface them when language suggests self-harm or crisis, and state plainly that Steady is a skills coach and not therapy or a diagnosis, with pointers to professional help for persistent difficulties.
- **Claims discipline:** describe Steady as helping users notice patterns and practice skills; avoid accuracy percentages, "clinically proven," or disorder-treatment claims unless backed by trials.
- **Validate across populations** (Hume Initiative principle) before launch, including neurodivergent users, non-native speakers and diverse skin tones.
